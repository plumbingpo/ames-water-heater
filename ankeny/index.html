Keeping Your Ankeny Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Ankeny

Most water heater failures in Ankeny announce themselves before they happen. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.
