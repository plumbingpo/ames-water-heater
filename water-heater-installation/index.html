Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • A large soaking tub that a standard tank cannot fill
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Installation?

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. A vent outside the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, or clearance can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. The plumbing side needs a correctly rated relief valve and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. You end up with an installation that is safe, properly sized, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • A periodic checkup with an anode inspection keeps a new tank healthy for years
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
