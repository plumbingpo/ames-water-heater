How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. On the gas side there is the supply line, the sediment trap, the shutoff, and a vent that has to draft properly so exhaust never backs up into the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. A correctly rated relief valve has to be piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the new heater to your household, confirm the gas line, vent, and electrical service can support it, and install every code item your home requires. Before leaving, we leak-test, verify draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature. It is what separates a heater that runs quietly for years from one that has problems from the start. And when a repair would give your current tank several more good years, we say so instead of selling you a new one.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
