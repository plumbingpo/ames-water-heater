How Nevada Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Hot Water Headaches Nevada Homeowners Face

Most water heater failures in Nevada give warning signs well before the tank gives out. Pay attention to rumbling and popping while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon.
