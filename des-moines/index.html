Keeping Your Des Moines Water Heater Running Longer

Most premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Des Moines

Whatever type of water heater your Des Moines home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
